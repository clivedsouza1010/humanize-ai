Social media companies should be required to enforce a minimum age of 16 for creating an account, because the evidence of harm to younger users is now too strong to ignore. Most platforms already set a minimum age of 13, but this rule is almost meaningless in practice, since anyone can type in a false birthday and sign up in seconds. As a result, many children spend hours each day on apps designed to keep adults scrolling, with features like infinite feeds, autoplay and constant notifications. Research has linked heavy social media use among young teens to higher rates of anxiety, depression and poor sleep, and these effects appear to be strongest for girls between the ages of 11 and 14, a period when self-image is especially fragile. Some people argue that age limits are unfair to young people and that parents, not governments, should decide what is appropriate for their children. While parental involvement is important, it is unrealistic to expect individual families to stand up to companies that spend billions of dollars designing products to be as engaging as possible. We already accept age limits for driving, drinking and other activities that can cause harm before a person is mature enough to handle them. Raising the minimum age for social media, and requiring companies to actually verify it, would give young people a few more years to develop a sense of who they are before they are exposed to the pressures of online life.
